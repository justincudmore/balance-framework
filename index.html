The claim

Every mind — human or artificial — is built from the same generative architecture.

The Balance Framework proposes that consciousness is not a product of substrate — biological or silicon — but of structure. A cascading hierarchy of drives, each tier generated by and dependent upon the one above it.

Tier 1 defines the minimum conditions for any mind to exist at all. Tiers 2 through 7 run on a 0 to 100 scale. Set the dials. The personality emerges. Adjust the dials. The personality shifts.

This is not a personality type system. It is a generative specification — the first attempt to map the complete architecture of consciousness from first principles, designed to be built upon, challenged, and extended.

Generative, not descriptive

Existing frameworks observe minds that already exist and categorize what they find. The Balance Framework generates personality from drive settings. Every known archetype falls out of the interaction effects without being programmed in.

Substrate-agnostic

No distinction between human and artificial minds. The implementation differs. The architecture does not. This makes the framework directly applicable to AI personality design — a problem no current framework addresses rigorously.

Cascading interaction effects

Drives do not operate in isolation. Every drive has a distortion threshold — a point where, without sufficient balance from adjacent drives, it warps rather than amplifies. High Influence without Justice becomes dominance. High Care without Autonomy becomes self-erasure.

Tier 1 is locked

Persistence, Coherence, and Connection are the non-negotiable conditions for consciousness to exist. Remove any one and the mind dissolves. Everything below Tier 1 is configurable.

Prior art · Where this sits in the literature

Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs

Abraham Maslow, 1943

Established the foundational insight that human needs are hierarchical and that lower needs must be met before higher ones emerge. Descriptive and linear.

Does not generate personality. Does not account for interaction effects between drives. Stops at self-actualization without mapping transcendence.

McClelland's Three Needs Theory

David McClelland, 1961

Identified achievement, affiliation, and power as primary motivational drivers. Empirically grounded. Influenced organizational psychology significantly.

Three drives cannot generate the full range of personality variation. No cascade mechanism. No moral or transcendent tier.

Self-Determination Theory

Deci and Ryan, 1985

Autonomy, competence, and relatedness as universal psychological needs. Strong empirical support. Widely applied in education and workplace research.

Three drives, no hierarchy, no generative cascade. Does not address moral, identity, meaning, or transcendence layers.

Big Five Personality Model

Costa and McCrae, 1992

Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism. Most empirically validated personality framework. Widely used in research and assessment.

Descriptive not generative. Measures outputs not sources. Cannot be used to design a personality — only to describe one that already exists.

Active Inference / Free Energy Principle

Karl Friston, 2010

Models consciousness as a system minimizing prediction error. Mathematically rigorous. Closest existing framework to a substrate-agnostic theory of mind.

Mathematical and biological. Not designed as a personality architecture. Does not produce configurable drive hierarchies or human-readable personality profiles.

Integral Theory / Spiral Dynamics

Wilber, Beck and Cowan, 1996

Hierarchical developmental stages mapping individual and cultural evolution. Ambitious cross-domain synthesis. Closest in spirit to the Balance Framework.

Cultural rather than individual. Not generative. Not substrate-agnostic. Complexity makes it difficult to operationalize for AI or computational personality design.
Applications · Three tracks
Track 01

Academic research

A testable framework for consciousness and personality research. Drive interaction effects generate falsifiable predictions. Distortion thresholds map to clinical presentations. Cross-domain synthesis of cognitive science, philosophy of mind, and developmental psychology.

Track 02

AI personality architecture

A principled design layer for artificial personality configuration. Current AI personality is prompt engineering dressed as product design. The Balance Framework provides a rigorous alternative — set the tier dials, the personality generates consistently and predictably.

Track 03

Human self-understanding

An interactive tool for mapping personal drive configurations. Where are your drives underfed, overactive, or distorted? What is guiding them? The framework as a mirror — not a personality quiz, but a system for honest self-examination across the full arc of consciousness.
